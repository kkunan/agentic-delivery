# Writing for people outside the team

These rules come from a product session that wrote a public roadmap with its owner over three days in October 2026. They add to the writing rules of the project. They do not replace them.

## The reader

- Write for a reader who knows nothing of the team. Words such as "release", "sprint", or "ticket" are jargon to that reader. Use the word that the reader uses.
- A reader takes an example as the whole set. Say "for example", and give an example that does not mislead.
- A public text holds no internal notes, no ticket numbers, and no team names for things.

## The order

- Get the reader to agree first. Name the problem in the words of the reader. Show numbers only after that. A score such as "4 of 7 covered" convinces only the team.
- Go slowly. Give one message in each paragraph.
- Link each section to the next one. Remind the reader of the point before, because people forget.

## Honesty

- Report progress as it is. If a part is half done, say that it is half done.
- Make no claim of success. Show what changed, and let the reader judge.
- Avoid AI phrasing: decorative groups of three, a clause that starts with ", and each", and a closing line that praises the work.

## A roadmap

- A roadmap shows what users get, and when. Give each period equal weight.
- A small feature does not go on a roadmap. Put it in the backlog.

## The review

- Show the draft to the owner before it goes public. A human reads text differently from an AI. Expect many small comments, and apply each one.
- Ask the owner one question in each reply.
