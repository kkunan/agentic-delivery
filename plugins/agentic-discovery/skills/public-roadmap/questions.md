# Public roadmap: template and owner questions

This folder holds a template for a public roadmap page, and the questions to ask the owner of the product. Both come from a public roadmap that took about 90 versions and three days of comments with its owner. The questions below try to get most of those answers in one talk.

## The files

- `template.html` is the page, with each piece of text as a `{{placeholder}}`. Colors `--p1`, `--p2` and `--p3` belong to the three pillars. Each pillar keeps its color on every part of the page.
- `questions.md` is this file.

## The page, top to bottom

1. The hero: one sentence on what the product is and the date of each part, a small timeline, and four facts.
2. Who we build for: three to seven example users, each with one real scene. No product yet.
3. The key value: a dark panel that tells how life changes for those same users. Its title names the key value of this product. For example, the title "Now imagine one place for all of them" fits a problem of scattered information. Other products have other values, for example a lower cost, a faster task, or a safer choice.
4. The plan on one page: the vision at the top, the feeling under it, three pillars, and the promises at the bottom. The drawing has a roof shape, but the text does not need a home theme.
5. The plan: three periods with equal weight. Each period says what it adds to each pillar, and names its partners.
6. How we know it works: three moments that map to conversion, engagement and returning users, with an honest "today" for each one. The page names each moment in the words of the reader, not with these three terms.
7. What it means for you: one box for each kind of reader, each with a contact.
8. The close: back to the users from the start, a button to the product, and a contact.

Each section ends with a short bridge to the next one. The reader forgets, so the bridge reminds the reader of the point before.

## How to run the talk

- Ask one question in each message. Wait for the answer before you ask the next one.
- Use the words of the owner and the users. Do not use team words such as "release", "sprint" or "epic".
- Write down the exact words of the owner. The best lines on the page often come from them.
- If an answer is vague, ask for one real person or one real scene.
- You do not need every answer in the first talk. Mark each gap, and draft the page with the gap visible.

## The questions

### A. The people (fills sections 1 and 2)

1. Who uses the product? Name the different kinds of people, not one average user.
2. For each kind of person: what question do they ask, in their own words?
3. For each kind of person: what happens to them today, without the product? Give one real scene.
4. What do all of them share, under their different questions?
5. Which of these people do you know in real life? Can the page use their story without a name?

### B. The key value (fills section 3)

6. Picture each of these people after the product exists. What is different for them on a normal day?
6a. What is the one value that all of them get? Put it in a few words. This becomes the title of the panel.
7. What do people need to feel safe to use it? For example, no account, no name, or no cost.

### C. The house (fills section 4)

8. In one sentence: what will the product become?
9. In one short line: what do you want every user to feel in the product?
10. What three things does the product say to every user? Write each one as a promise to the user, for example "Your money, always in view".
11. For each promise: what is life like before it, and after it?
12. Which kinds of people does each promise help most?
13. What promises does the product never break? For example, price, privacy, ads, or who owns the content.
14. What keeps the product running: partners, money, or people?

### D. The plan (fills sections 1 and 5)

15. What do you split the plan by: years, quarters, or seasons? Use what the reader understands.
16. What is live today? Be honest about what is half done.
17. For each period: who does it help most, and what is its theme in five words?
18. For each period: what does it add to each of the three promises?
19. For each period: which partners do you need?
20. Which items are too small for a public page? Those go to the backlog.
21. What happens after the last period? The page must not say that the work is done.

### E. How we know it works (fills section 6)

The three moments map to three product measures:

- Conversion: the user gets the value for the first time.
- Engagement: the user does something with it that moves them forward.
- Returning: when the user needs it again, the user comes back.

22. For your product, what is the moment of each measure? Describe it as something that happens to the user.
23. For each moment: if it fails, what happens to the user?
24. For each moment: where are we today, honestly?
25. For each moment: how do you hope it grows in each period?
26. What numbers do you refuse to count, and why? For example, downloads or time in the app.
27. When do you next look at these moments, and what do you decide then?

Made-up samples, to help the owner answer:

| Product | Conversion | Engagement | Returning |
|---|---|---|---|
| A tutoring app for students | She finds a tutor who fits her subject and budget. | She books a second lesson, and shares her notes with the tutor. | She comes back next term, for a new subject. |
| A savings app for young workers | He sets his first savings goal. | He moves money to the goal each payday. | He comes back to set a new goal after he reaches the first one. |
| A booking site for a small clinic | A patient books a first visit online. | The patient fills in the health form before the visit. | The patient books the follow-up visit without a phone call. |

### F. The readers (fills sections 7 and 8)

28. Who reads this page besides the users? For example, families, partners, funders, or people who want to help.
29. For each reader: what do they get, what do you ask of them, and what do you promise them?
30. Which email address or link takes questions?
31. Where does the button at the end go?
32. Which facts about the team are public? For example, how many people, how many hours, and how it is paid for.

## Before the page goes public

Read the draft against these rules, then give it to the owner for comments.

- The reader agrees with the problem before seeing any number.
- The examples say that they are examples, not everyone.
- Each "today" line is honest. Half done says half done.
- Each period has about equal weight.
- The page holds no ticket numbers, team names for things, or internal notes.
- The page makes no claim of success. It shows the change, and the reader judges.
- No decorative groups of three. No ", and each". No closing line that praises the work.
- Each section has a bridge to the next one.
- Each reader box has a contact.

Expect many small comments from the owner. A person reads text differently from an AI. Apply each comment, one at a time.
